Sewer backup in the house right now

Raw sewage carries bacteria, so treat any spill as a health matter. These steps limit the damage until help arrives.

Stop adding water

No flushing, laundry, dishwasher or long showers. Every gallon has nowhere to go but out of the lowest drain.

Keep people out

Children and pets stay out of the room. Wear rubber gloves and boots if you must go in.

Shut off power if water is near outlets

Use the breaker panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground.

Find the cleanout

Look for a capped pipe, usually 3 or 4 inches wide, near the foundation or in the yard. Tell the dispatcher where it is.

After-hours and same-day visits are available when the schedule allows, and the dispatcher makes no promise about arrival time. Gas smell anywhere? Leave and call the gas utility or 911 first.

What a main line cleaning does

The main sewer line is the single pipe, normally 3 or 4 inches across, that collects waste from the whole house and runs to the city mainline. When it clogs, the lowest fixtures fail first, which is why a basement or ground-level shower often shows trouble before the sinks do.

A plumber opens the cleanout and feeds in a motorized cable with a cutting head. That chews through roots, grease and packed paper. For heavy buildup, a hydro jet sends water at several thousand psi to scour the pipe wall, which leaves it cleaner than a cable alone.

Cleaning clears the blockage. It does not tell you why it formed, which is where a camera comes in.

Your lateral, the city mainline and Mauldin's red clay

According to the City Public Works Committee, the city system consists only of mainlines and manholes in street rights-of-way and easements. The lateral from house to mainline belongs to the property owner. So a clog inside your property line is yours to clear, and that is the work we do.

If sewage is coming up from a manhole in the street, or several neighbors are backed up at once, the problem is probably the city's mainline. Call Mauldin Public Works at (864) 289-8904, or (864) 289-8900 after hours. Reporting early helps even if your own line is fine.

Red clay soils are common across the South Carolina Piedmont, according to the USDA Cecil series description. Clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and that movement can shift older pipe joints and give roots a gap to enter. A backup that keeps returning may be a joint problem, not just grease.

Warning signs that a main line is closing up

A main line seldom fails without hints. Watch for these before the floor drain overflows.

  • ✓Gurgling in a tub or sink when the toilet flushes.
  • ✓Two or more fixtures draining slowly at the same time.
  • ✓Sewer smell near a floor drain or in the yard.
  • ✓Water rising in a shower when the washer drains.
  • ✓Soggy, unusually green patches over the sewer route.

After the cleaning: preventing the next backup

If the clog was grease or paper, changing habits may be enough. Keep fats out of the kitchen drain and flush only toilet paper.

If roots were the cause, they grow back, often within a year or two. A camera inspection of the line shows where they enter, and regular cleaning can then be timed before they close the pipe again. Kitchen-only slowdowns may only need a smaller drain cleaning.
