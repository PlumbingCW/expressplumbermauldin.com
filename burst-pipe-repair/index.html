Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Mauldin, SC

If a pipe is spraying or pouring water in your Mauldin, SC home, shut off the main valve first, then call our 24/7 line. Burst pipe repair means stopping the flow, cutting out the split section and joining new pipe in its place. Open a low faucet to drain the lines, switch off power to wet rooms, and move valuables away from the water.

Most burst calls come from a cracked section, a failed supply valve under a sink, or a joint that let go. All three can be repaired once the water is off.

Water gushing right now: the first ten minutes

Do these in this order. They cut the damage while the plumber is on the way.

  1. 1

    Close the main shutoff

    Look where the line enters the house, in the garage, crawlspace, basement or a yard box. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops.

  2. 2

    Drain the lines

    Open the lowest faucet, then one upstairs. Pressure bleeds off and the flow slows.

  3. 3

    Kill power to wet areas

    Flip the breakers for rooms with standing water. Do not step into a flooded room with outlets live.

  4. 4

    Switch off the water heater

    An empty tank can burn out an element. Turn off the gas control or breaker.

  5. 5

    Photograph and mop up

    Take pictures for your insurer, then lift rugs and run towels. Wet drywall starts to sag within a day.

Why pipes split in a mild climate like ours

NWS Greenville-Spartanburg lists an average January low of 32.1 F and 16 days at or below 32 F. That is enough for ice to form in an exposed line in a garage, an attic or an outside wall. Ice does not burst the pipe where it forms. Pressure builds between the ice plug and a closed faucet, and the split opens in the pipe wall.

Freezing is not the only cause. Old fittings corrode, supply valves under sinks seize and snap when turned, and water pressure above about 80 psi strains joints. Ask the plumber to check pressure after any burst.

What the January 2018 cold spell tells a Mauldin homeowner

On January 3 and 4, 2018, a cold spell broke water mains in Greenville (Academy Street and West Washington Street) and in Greer (Pelham Road and East James Street). In Greer more than 100 homes lost water for a time and a boil water advisory lasted several hours. WSPA's report does not mention Mauldin.

Those were utility mains, not house pipes. The lesson is practical: if your whole street loses pressure, call your water utility. If water is pouring inside your walls while neighbors are fine, the break is on your side, and it is your repair.

After the water is off: what to check before the plumber arrives

  • ✓Find the break or the wettest spot and note its location.
  • ✓Check ceilings under the area for bulges and sag.
  • ✓Note whether the water hammered or squealed before it failed.
  • ✓Check other exposed lines in the garage or attic for ice.
  • ✓List wet rooms for your insurance claim.

If a ceiling holds a bulge of water, poke a small hole and catch it in a bucket, since a controlled drain beats a sudden collapse. A leak detection visit is the next step when a stain appears where no pipe is visible.

Repair a section or redo the line

One burst in an otherwise sound line calls for a section repair. That is the right choice for newer PEX or copper that failed because of a freeze or a bad fitting. Skip a full replacement there, because it costs far more than the problem warrants.

Two splits in one season are a different story. They point to corroded pipe or high pressure, and a longer-term plan saves repeat flooding. Ask for both options in writing before work starts.

What drives the cost of a burst pipe repair
