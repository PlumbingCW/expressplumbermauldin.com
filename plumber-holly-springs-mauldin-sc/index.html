Holly Springs at a glance

Holly Springs is reached from Bridges Road, close to the I-385 interchange. Streets that appear in listing records include Brookbend Road, Brookbend Court, Poplar Lane, Holly Lane, Beechwood Court, Blackgum Court, Springvale Drive and Old Bridges Road. A Census address check places Brookbend Road inside Mauldin city limits.

Real-estate listing data says homes were built between 1972 and 1998, with 1972 the median year. Most are single-family ranch and split-level houses, with some two-story and tri-level homes, and brick exteriors are common. Lots run from about 0.29 to 0.79 acre and average roughly 0.44 acre.

The same source records no community-level homeowners association. For a plumbing problem, that means the whole house and the lot belong to the owner, and no association stands between you and the repair. Mauldin High School and a city park are within walking distance according to listing data.

A neighborhood built across 26 years

The build years in Holly Springs span more than two decades. A house finished in 1972 and one finished in 1998 can sit a few streets apart, so the age of the plumbing varies from address to address. Nobody can say from the neighborhood name alone what is inside a particular wall.

The practical step is to find out about your own house. A home inspection report, closing documents or records of past permits show what has been replaced and when. If a previous owner remodeled a bathroom or moved a water heater, that work may be older or newer than the house itself.

Four things to find before you call

  • ✓The main water shutoff valve for the house, and whether it turns freely
  • ✓The Greenville Water meter at the street, and the lid that covers it
  • ✓The rating label on the water heater, which shows its manufacture date
  • ✓The sewer cleanout cap in the yard or near the foundation, if your house has one

Knowing these four locations lets you stop the water quickly and gives a plumber something specific to work with when you call.

Leaks, drains and heaters in a no-HOA neighborhood

With no association involved, an owner can book a plumber directly for any problem on the property. Slow or repeating clogs are the job for drain cleaning, and a line that keeps backing up after a cleaning may need a camera look through sewer inspection.

Water pooling in the yard or a bill that suddenly climbs points to leak detection. A tank that leaks at the base or no longer heats may need water heater repair or replacement. For problems around Bridges Road and I-385, the Mauldin plumbing page has the full list of services.

Meter test, sewer lateral and permits

Greenville Water explains a simple leak check. Read the meter, wait at least 15 minutes without using any water, and read it again. If the numbers moved, water is escaping somewhere. A toilet dye test, using food coloring in the tank and no flush for an hour, finds a leaking flapper.

A sewer backup in the street or a manhole overflow belongs to City of Mauldin Public Works at (864) 289-8904, or (864) 289-8900 after hours. A backup that starts inside the house may involve the lateral, which the owner maintains.

A permit is required before you add, alter or replace any plumbing system in the city. Building and Zoning is at 5 E. Butler Rd, (864) 234-3475. Ask whoever does the work who pulls the permit.
