Greenville's historic districts and the pipes inside them

The city lists several historic districts, and each one grew in a different decade. West End was designated a historic district in 1993, with settlement there as early as the 1830s. East Park Avenue developed in the early 1900s, and Overbrook grew along the trolley line.

Hampton-Pinckney, Heritage, Colonel Elias Earle and Pettigru round out the list, with Pettigru alone holding 88 structures. If your house sits in one of these, assume the original drain lines may be cast iron or clay and the supply may have been replaced at least once. A camera and a pressure test replace guessing.

Ask the city's Building & Permit Center whether a district rule touches your project before any wall is opened.

Why one wet spot can mean two different repairs in Greenville

In an old district, a damp slab or ceiling stain can come from a failing drain, a pinhole in galvanized supply, or a tank leak. Electronic listening and a pressure drop test separate these before any wall is opened. Skipping that step is how a repair gets done twice. Leak detection first, repair second.

Greenville Water, the meter line and backflow testers

Greenville is served by Greenville Water, a separate commission from the city. It draws from Table Rock Reservoir, North Saluda Reservoir and Lake Keowee. The utility maintains the line up to the outlet side of the meter, and the line leaving the meter is the customer's to maintain.

That rule decides who pays for a buried leak between the meter and the house. A reading before and after 15 minutes without water use tells you whether the meter is moving. If it moves with everything off, the problem is on your side, and water service line repair is the next call.

Greenville Water also runs a backflow program. Customers whose water presents a backflow hazard must install a device, and devices must be tested every year. The utility does not install or test them, so a certified tester does the work and submits the result through the utility portal. Commercial sites should read the commercial plumbing page.

After a main break, your own pipes may act up

Greenville Water has had system events. A December 2018 break near the North Saluda River left about 700 customers under a boil advisory that lifted December 30. A November 2023 break on a 72-inch main near the Adkins plant led to a voluntary conservation notice for all customers. A January 2018 cold spell broke mains on Academy St and W. Washington St.

After any of these, run the cold taps for a few minutes. Air and sediment can clog aerators and make toilets sputter. Persistent low flow afterward is worth a call, and a repipe of tired supply lines may be the lasting fix in older houses.
