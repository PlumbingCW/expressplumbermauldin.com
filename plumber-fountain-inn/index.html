The mill village houses on First to Fifth Streets

Woodside Mill Village was created about 1910 with 67 houses on First through Fifth Streets and Woodside Avenue. A state survey in 1999 found that of 146 historic dwellings it counted in town, 136 were built in or after 1900, mostly in the early to mid twentieth century.

If you own one of these, the drains are likely old and the original supply may be gone or galvanized. Ask a plumber to scope the main line before you buy or remodel. Tree roots find old joints easily, and a sewer camera inspection shows the pipe's real condition before you commit money.

Sewage or water on the floor: what to do in Fountain Inn right now

The emergency line has no closing hour. Here is what helps while a plumber heads your way.

Stop adding water

No flushing, laundry or dishwasher. A backed-up main fills the lowest drain first, usually a floor drain or tub.

Switch off the water heater

If the heater sits in a flooded closet or garage, cut its breaker or gas control first.

Keep kids and pets out

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep the area closed off and wear gloves and boots if you must go in.

Smell gas? Leave

Call the gas utility or 911 from outside. A plumber comes after the house is cleared.

Fountain Inn sewer, permits and the water question

The city runs its own sewer, and its fee schedule has a Public Works section for sewer taps. If your backup is in the street, call the city. If the stoppage is inside your house or in your own line, a plumber handles it.

For water, the town straddles Greenville and Laurens counties. Ask the city or check your water bill to see which utility serves your address, because the answer decides who to call about a meter or main.

Permits go through the Planning & Development department and its Permit Center on fountaininn.org. A water heater swap or a sewer repair may need one, so ask first.

How to sort out a drain problem in order

A short routine saves a trip if the cause is simple.

  1. 1

    Try the nearest plunger

    If only one fixture is slow, a plunger often clears it. Pull the stopper and clean it first.

  2. 2

    Test a second fixture

    Run the tub and the washer drain. If two fixtures gurgle or back up, the main line is involved.

  3. 3

    Call and describe the pattern

    Tell dispatch which fixtures are affected. That narrows the cause before arrival.

  4. 4

    Authorize a camera run

    A camera finds roots, a broken joint or a belly. Repair options come after you see the picture.
