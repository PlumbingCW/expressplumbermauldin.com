Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Mauldin, SC

A water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the Greenville Water meter to your house, and a break in it is an emergency in Mauldin, SC. Dispatch runs 24/7. While you wait, close the house main shutoff and keep everyone away from soft, sinking ground.

This page explains who owns the line, how a break is found without digging up the yard, and when a repair beats a full replacement.

You own the pipe once it leaves the meter

Greenville Water maintains the line up to the outlet side of the meter. Greenville Water states that the line leaving the meter is the customer's service line, and the customer maintains it. A wet patch past the meter is therefore your repair, not a utility call.

That ownership line matters when you are deciding fast. If the meter box itself is leaking before the shutoff, call Greenville Water at (864) 241-6000. If water rises anywhere beyond it, a plumber is the right call.

Confirm a buried leak in four checks

These checks come from Greenville Water's own guidance and take about half an hour.

  1. 1

    Stop all water use

    Close taps, check that no toilet is running and make sure the ice maker and irrigation are idle.

  2. 2

    Read the meter

    Note the dial, wait at least 15 minutes with no water used, then read it again.

  3. 3

    Close the house cutoff

    Shut the main valve at the house and watch the outdoor meter.

  4. 4

    Read the result

    If the meter still runs with the house cutoff closed, the leak is in the line between meter and house.

The line has burst and water is surfacing

A pressurized 3/4 inch or 1 inch line can move a lot of water through a small crack, much like a burst pipe inside the house. Treat a fast-growing wet spot as urgent.

Close the main shutoff

Turn it off inside the house. This stops flow to everything downstream of the break.

Keep clear of the spot

Saturated clay can slump, and water can undermine a driveway edge or a walk.

Do not run a hose to flush it

Adding water makes the washout larger and hides the source.

Fill a few containers first

Store water for drinking and toilets before the main goes off for repair.

Repair, replace or reroute the line

The right choice depends on pipe material, length and how many breaks the line has had.

OptionBest forTrade-off
Spot repair with a couplingOne clean break in sound pipeCheap and quick, but the next weak point may be a few feet away
Trench and replace the runOld galvanized or repeated leaksDigs up lawn and paving, yet it ends the problem for decades
Trenchless pipe pullingLines under driveways, walks or rootsNeeds entry and exit pits and suitable soil
Reroute a new lineLines that run under a slab or additionCosts more and changes the meter-to-house path

Do not choose replacement for one pinhole in a new copper or PEX line. A coupling is the honest repair.

How an older Mauldin line fails

Greenville Water lines reached Laurens Road in 1953, and US 276 was built through the center of Mauldin in 1956. Homes here went up over several decades, so service lines are a mix of materials: galvanized steel, copper, polyethylene and PVC. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside and loses flow long before it leaks.

So ask what the line is made of. If the whole house has old galvanized pipe, a repipe of the house plumbing may belong on the same quote. A plumber who traces the pipe at the meter can tell, and the answer decides whether a patch holds. Red clay soils are common across the SC Piedmont, and clay holds water against a leak and hides its path to the surface.

Signs the buried line is leaking

  • ✓The meter dial moves with every fixture off.
  • ✓A section of lawn stays soggy in dry weather.
  • ✓Water pressure drops through the whole house at once.
  • ✓You hear a hiss near the entry wall or the meter.
  • ✓The monthly bill climbs with no change in habits.

Any one of these deserves the meter test. Two or more together call for a pinpoint leak survey before anyone digs.

What changes water service line repair cost

Typical U.S. costs run roughly $500 to $1,500 for a short spot repair and $1,500 to $5,000 or more to replace a line. The real number depends on the job and comes from a call.

Length of lineA 40 foot run costs less than a 120 foot run, and the distance is measured at the visit.
Depth and soilDigging red clay or rock slows the work.
Surface above the pipeLawn restores cheaply. Concrete, pavers and driveways do not.
Pipe materialPEX and copper cost differently, and the choice follows what suits your pressure and soil.
Hour of the visitAfter-hours work is possible, and any difference is stated before work starts.

Ask your insurer whether the line is covered, since policies differ. A broken line is also grounds to check slab leak repair if pipes run under the floor.

Permits for a Mauldin service line job

The City of Mauldin says a permit is required before you add, alter or replace any plumbing system. Contractors must be licensed, bonded and insured, and have the permit before starting. Ask the Building and Zoning office at 5 E. Butler Rd, (864) 234-3475, whether your line work needs one.

That is why an unpermitted dig by a handyman is a risk. Hiring a licensed plumber also gives you a person to call if the repair fails.
