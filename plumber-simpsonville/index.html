A city that doubled down on growth, and what that means for your pipes

Simpsonville became a post office in 1886 and was incorporated in 1901. Growth was slow until the 1950s, then fast. It ranked as the state's fastest-growing city from 1990 to 1992, and the Census counted 23,354 residents in 2020 and an estimated 28,459 in July 2025.

That history matters under your sink. A house from the 1950s, one from 1995 and one from 2022 are three different plumbing systems, even if they share a street name and a ZIP code. We dispatch all of them around the clock from Mauldin, and the first question on the phone is usually the age of the house.

If you live in 29681, the larger of the two ZIPs, your mailing address may even say Simpsonville while the house sits beside Mauldin. Either way, tell the dispatcher the house age and the ZIP, and the call goes faster.

What Simpsonville has published about its sewer pipes

The city says its sewer infrastructure has exceeded its useful life, and it describes clay sewer pipes and brick manholes installed more than half a century ago. A $10 million infrastructure grant was announced in 2023.

For you, that means older streets have older mains, and roots and ground movement hit clay joints first. If a drain backs up in several fixtures at once, a camera run shows whether the blockage is on your side of the property line. A sewer camera inspection answers that before anyone digs.

Permits go through the Planning and Economic Development Department at (864) 967-9526, and the city has an online permit portal. Ask them whether your repair needs a permit.

Calls from the older streets near the mill history

The Simpsonville Cotton Mill dates to 1907, and houses from that era often have cast iron drains and galvanized supply pipe. Steel supply pipe corrodes from the inside, so pressure falls long before a leak appears. If a faucet that ran fine five years ago now trickles, a repipe of the supply lines is worth pricing.

Repiping is a big job and not always the right first step. If only one run is bad, replacing that section costs far less. A plumber should show you where the pipe is bad before suggesting more.

Leak, flood or backup in Simpsonville: do this before the plumber arrives

The 24/7 line answers day and night. While you wait, these four moves limit the damage.

Find the main shutoff

Most houses have a valve near the meter or where the supply enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Kill power to wet areas

If water reaches outlets, appliances or a garage freezer, switch off that breaker. Do not stand in water to reach the panel.

Clear the floor

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the spill path. Towels at doorways slow the spread.

Smell gas? Leave first

Go outside and call the gas utility or 911. Plumbing can wait until the house is safe.
