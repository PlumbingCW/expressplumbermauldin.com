Leaking water under the floor right now and no puddle to show for it

A slab leak does not announce itself with a puddle. It shows up as a warm patch of tile, a meter that spins with every fixture off, or a faint hiss at night. If that sounds like your house, treat it as urgent and call the 24/7 line at (855) 575-2890. Same-day and after-hours visits are offered when the schedule allows.

While you wait, turn off the main shutoff where the line enters the house. Open one faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines, and switch off the water heater breaker or gas control so the tank does not run dry and overheat.

Signs the leak is under the slab

One sign alone can mean something simpler. Two or three together point down.

  • ✓A warm or hot spot on the floor, often from a leaking hot line.
  • ✓The sound of running water when every tap and appliance is off.
  • ✓Water meter dial moving after you shut off every fixture for at least 15 minutes.
  • ✓A sudden jump in the water bill with no change in use.
  • ✓Damp carpet, lifting laminate, or a musty smell near a wall base.
  • ✓New cracks in tile or drywall, or low pressure at every faucet.

A meter test can separate a slab leak from a toilet running in the dark. Leak detection pins down the spot before anyone cuts concrete.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • ✓Shut off the main water valve if you hear running water or the meter keeps moving
  • ✓Turn off the water heater at the breaker or gas control so it does not run dry
  • ✓Keep power tools, cords and appliances away from any wet floor
  • ✓Move rugs and furniture off warm or damp spots so the slab can be checked
  • ✓Take a photo of the meter reading and the time to show the plumber
  • ✓If you smell gas or see water near an outlet, leave the area and call 911 or the gas utility

How the leak gets located without guessing

Plumbers use a few tools together. A pressure test isolates which line is losing water, and an electronic listening device picks up the sound of the leak through the floor. A thermal camera can show a hot line, and a tracer gas or line locator confirms the path of the pipe.

The aim is a hole the size of a dinner plate, not a trench across the living room. Marking the pipe route first also protects other lines and electrical conduit in the slab.

Mauldin's red clay and 47 inches of rain

The USDA Cecil series description, which covers red clay soils common across the South Carolina Piedmont, lists a clay layer roughly 8 to 26 inches deep. NWS Greenville-Spartanburg lists annual precipitation at about 47 inches. That does not mean every Mauldin lot has the same soil, so a plumber should check yours.

Why it matters to a leak: clay drains slowly, and wet soil under a slab holds water instead of letting it pass. Leaked water can travel along the bottom of the concrete and surface a room away. That is why the leak is located by instrument first, and the dig goes where the tool says.

When not to jackhammer

If the leak is on a cold line in the middle of a floor you can reroute around, going overhead often costs less than breaking and re-pouring concrete. If the line is on a hot line to a water heater, the cause may be a failing water heater connection and not a slab leak at all. Ask the plumber to show the test result before any cutting begins.
