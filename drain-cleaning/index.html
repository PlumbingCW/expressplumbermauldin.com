Water standing in the tub or sink right now

Every gallon you add to a blocked line has to go somewhere, and it usually comes out of the lowest drain in the house. These four steps limit the mess until a plumber reaches you.

Stop adding water

Pause the dishwasher, washing machine and showers. Do not flush a toilet that is already slow.

Test every fixture

Run a little water in the farthest sink. If the shower floor or a floor drain bubbles, the blockage is in the main line.

Leave the drain opener on the shelf

Caustic cleaner can sit in a full pipe for hours and splash when the trap is opened.

Protect the floor

If wastewater is rising, lift rugs, keep children and pets out, and put towels at the doorway.

Signs the blockage is in the main drain

Branch clogs and main-line clogs need different tools, so it helps to know which you have before the plumber arrives.

  • ✓Two or more fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • ✓A toilet flush makes the tub or shower floor gurgle.

  • ✓A sewage smell comes from a floor drain or cleanout.
  • ✓The kitchen sink is fine but the downstairs bathroom is not.

Snaking, jetting and when to skip the bigger job

Snaking, which plumbers also call cabling, spins a steel cable through the pipe and breaks the clog apart. It is the right first move for a single-fixture clog and costs the least. It is the wrong move for a pipe that is cracked or sagging, because the cable can snag and the clog keeps returning.

Hydro jetting uses water at several thousand psi to scour grease off the pipe wall. Skip it for a first-time bathroom clog, where a cable does the job. Choose it for a kitchen line that fills up every spring or when a camera shows heavy buildup. If a camera shows a collapsed section, jetting only delays a sewer line repair.
