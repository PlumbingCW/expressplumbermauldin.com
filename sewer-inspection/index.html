Sewer Inspection in Mauldin, SC

A sewer inspection in Mauldin, SC is a plumber feeding a waterproof camera through your sewer lateral to see the pipe from the inside. You get live video of cracks, roots, sags and joints, so repair decisions rest on what is actually down there.

When a camera look at the sewer is worth paying for

Most people book an inspection for one of these reasons.

  • ✓You are buying a house and want to know the lateral's condition before closing.
  • ✓A main line has backed up twice in a year.
  • ✓Cleaning cleared the clog but you want to know why it formed.
  • ✓You see a sinkhole or soft green patch in the yard.
  • ✓The home has older clay, cast iron or fiber pipe.
  • ✓A repair quote arrived and you want proof before paying for a dig.

How the camera works and what it can show

The camera head is about 1.5 to 2 inches across and rides on a flexible push rod with lights and a distance counter. It enters through a cleanout and travels toward the street while a monitor records the run.

Many cameras also carry a transmitter. A locator above ground picks up its signal, so the plumber can mark the depth and path of the pipe on your lawn. That tells you where a dig would have to happen.

A camera cannot see through standing water or a heavy blockage. If the pipe is full, the plumber cleans it first, which is a separate step.

Who owns what: Mauldin's lateral rule and who to hire

A City Public Works Committee agenda from October 2024 says the city system consists only of mainlines and manholes in street rights-of-way and easements. The lateral from the house to the mainline belongs to and is maintained by the property owner. A camera inspection of your lateral therefore covers a pipe you are responsible for, and the video is your evidence.

Before hiring anyone for repairs after an inspection, check the license. South Carolina residential plumbers are licensed through the Residential Builders Commission under LLR, which offers a public lookup. Commercial work over $10,000 goes through the Contractors' Licensing Board.

Sellers and buyers should agree on who pays for any repair the video finds. That is a contract matter, so ask your agent or attorney.

Source type: City of Mauldin Public Works Committee and SC LLR.

Not every defect means you should dig

A small root intrusion or a mild belly can be managed with scheduled cleaning for years. Digging a trench costs several times as much and tears up lawn, driveway or slab.

Replace or reline when the video shows a break, a collapse or a belly that holds water and keeps causing backups. If a second opinion on your findings is useful, the saved video makes that easy.

What a sewer inspection costs

Typical U.S. prices run about $150 to $500 for a standard residential camera inspection. The real number comes from a call, and these factors move it:

Cleanout accessA clear exterior cleanout is quicker than pulling a toilet.
Pipe lengthA long run from the house to the street takes more time.
Cleaning needed firstA line full of grease or roots must be cleared before the camera can see.
Locating serviceMarking the depth and path above ground adds time and cost.
Written reportA recorded video and a written summary may cost more than a live view only.
