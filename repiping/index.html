Planned pipe replacement

Repiping in Mauldin, SC

Repiping in Mauldin, SC means replacing the water supply pipes inside a house, usually all of them, with new copper or PEX. It fits homes with galvanized steel, repeated pinhole leaks or rusty water at every tap.

This is planned work. You pick a day, compare materials and hear a clear price before any wall is opened. If a pipe fails first, the 24/7 line is open at (855) 575-2890.

What a repipe replaces and what it leaves alone

A repipe swaps the pressurized supply lines that feed sinks, showers, toilets, the water heater and hose bibs. It does not touch the drain lines that carry waste out. Those are a separate system with different materials and different failure modes.

Most whole-house jobs on a single-story house take two to four days. Walls are opened with small access holes, not torn out. PEX is flexible and snakes through framing, so patches are often a few inches to a foot across. Ask what finish work is included.

Signs your supply pipes are near the end

Check exposed pipe in a garage, basement or under a sink first. A magnet sticks to galvanized steel but not to copper or plastic.

  • ✓Water runs brown or orange after sitting overnight, then clears after a minute.
  • ✓Pressure is weak at every fixture, because rust narrows galvanized pipe from the inside.
  • ✓Pinhole leaks keep appearing in new spots on copper, often with green or white crust at joints.
  • ✓A second leak shows up within months of fixing the first.
  • ✓Gray, threaded pipe that a magnet grabs is galvanized steel, and it is rarely sound past 60 years.

Cracked or broken pipe: patch it or repipe?

A cracked or broken pipe is the most common reason people ask about repiping. One failure rarely justifies a whole-house job. If the rest of the pipe is sound, a plumber cuts out the damaged section and splices in new pipe for a fraction of the cost. If water is pouring right now, shut the main first and read about stopping a burst pipe.

The answer changes when the break is a symptom. Galvanized pipe that splits at one threaded joint is usually rusted at the rest. Copper with its third pinhole in a year is telling you about the whole run. A pressure test and a look inside a cut-out section show which case you have: clean inside means repair the spot, crusted solid means plan the repipe.

What Mauldin's history and rules mean for a repipe

Mauldin grew as its utilities arrived. Greenville Water lines reached Laurens Road in 1953, and US 276 was built through the center of town in 1956, per the city's own history. Houses from that first wave can still have steel supply pipe, but many were piped in copper, so a house's age proves nothing. Look at the pipe itself.

A repipe replaces a plumbing system, and the City of Mauldin Building and Zoning office states that a permit is required before you add, alter or replace one. Contractors must be licensed, bonded and insured and hold the permit before starting. Ask to see it before the first cut.

Code applies as well. Mauldin enforces the 2021 South Carolina Plumbing Code (or the 2021 International Plumbing Code with SC modifications) locally, since January 1, 2023. The 2024 codes are scheduled for January 1, 2027, so a job that slips toward that date is worth asking about.

How a whole-house repipe usually runs

  1. 1

    Walk the house

    The plumber traces the existing lines, counts fixtures and picks a route. Attic and crawlspace access save the most wall cutting.

  2. 2

    Run the new lines

    New PEX or copper goes to each fixture, with shutoff valves added where the old system had none.

  3. 3

    Pressure test

    The system is filled and held under test pressure while every joint and the gauge are checked.

  4. 4

    Inspect and patch

    If your permit calls for an inspection, it happens before walls are closed. Access holes are patched after.

Cast iron drain pipe is a different job

Cast iron was the standard material for home drain lines in houses built up to roughly the 1970s, and it can last 50 to 75 years. Inside, it grows rough scale that catches paper and grease, so slow drains and repeat clogs are the warning. Cracks and rust-through at joints show up in crawlspaces and under slabs.

A sewer camera inspection shows how bad the scale is. If the pipe is rough but sound, hydro jetting can clear it, or a liner can be cured inside it. If it is cracked, collapsed or offset, sections are cut out and replaced with PVC or ABS. Lining is the wrong call for a collapsed pipe.

What changes the price of a repipe

Whole-house repipes in the U.S. typically run about $4,000 to $15,000. Small single-story homes sit at the low end and large two-story homes with hard access at the high end. The real number comes from a call and a look at your house.
