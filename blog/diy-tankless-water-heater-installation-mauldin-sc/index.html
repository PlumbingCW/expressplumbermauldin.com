Installing a Tankless Water Heater Yourself: What Is Safe

By the Mauldin Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

Short answer for Mauldin, SC homeowners: the water connections are DIY-sized, but the gas, venting, and electrical work are where mistakes hurt people. Check permit and licensing rules with the city before you buy the unit.

Gas smell or alarm: stop first

If you smell gas, leave the house at once. Do not flip switches or use a phone inside. From outside, call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. A carbon monoxide alarm that sounds means the same thing: get everyone outside and call 911.

Why gas and venting are the real danger

A gas tankless heater burns a lot of fuel in a small chamber and sends the exhaust out through a vent pipe. If a joint is loose, the pipe is the wrong material, or the termination is too close to a window, exhaust can enter the home. Carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless, which is why detectors matter.

A gas connection has to be tight, tested, and sized for the load. Many existing tank heaters sit on a half inch line that is too small for a tankless unit. A homeowner can tighten a fitting and still miss the real flaw, which is the pipe size or a slow leak at a thread.

Licensing and code rules in Mauldin

South Carolina licenses residential plumbers through the Residential Builders Commission, which sits under the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR). LLR runs a public residential license lookup. Commercial plumbing work over $10,000 falls under the SC Contractors' Licensing Board.

For code, South Carolina adopted the 2021 SC Plumbing Code, or the 2021 International Plumbing Code with SC modifications, and local enforcement has been required since January 1, 2023. The 2024 SC codes have a scheduled effective date of January 1, 2027. If your install slips into that window, the plumber should know which edition applies.

The City of Mauldin Building and Zoning office states that a permit is required before you add, alter, or replace any plumbing system, and that contractors must be licensed, bonded, and insured. Whether a homeowner may pull their own permit for a tankless install is not something this guide can confirm. Ask the Mauldin Building and Zoning office at (864) 234-3475 before you buy.

Source type: SC LLR licensing pages, SC Building Codes Council, and City of Mauldin Building and Zoning.

Mistakes that show up on DIY units

The usual errors are small and costly. A relief valve discharge pipe gets capped, a vent joint is pushed together without sealing, or the unit is hung on a wall that cannot carry about 40 to 60 pounds. Some installers also skip the sediment trap, a short capped section of gas pipe that catches debris before it reaches the burner.

Another common miss is sizing. A unit that cannot raise incoming water enough for two showers gives lukewarm water, and no amount of tinkering fixes a unit that is too small. A site visit by a licensed plumber catches that before you pay for the wrong box.

What can go wrong with an unpermitted install

An install done without a required permit can cause problems when you sell the house or file an insurance claim. Insurance coverage varies by policy, so ask your carrier how it treats unpermitted work. The honest risk is not only a fine. It is a hidden gas or vent defect that nobody inspected.

Manufacturers also write warranty terms around installation. Some require a licensed installer, and some void coverage for errors. Read the paper that comes in the box before you start.

If you only want to help, do these safely

  • ✓Clear the space around the mounting wall.
  • ✓Flush the new unit's isolation valves yearly after install.

Planned work versus a failed tank

A tank that is leaking is a different situation. Shut off the water at the heater and the power or gas, then call. A planned swap gives you time to read the manual, but it does not change who must handle gas and venting.
