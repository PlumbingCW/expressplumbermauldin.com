Adding a Switch for Your Garbage Disposal, Safely

By the Mauldin Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

To install a garbage disposal switch in Mauldin, SC, you pick one of three routes: use an existing switched outlet, add an air switch on the countertop, or wire a new wall switch. The first two are homeowner-friendly. New wall wiring is electrician work.

Safety first: kill the breaker, then test

Turn off the breaker for the kitchen circuit before you touch any wire, and confirm the wires are dead with a non-contact voltage tester. A wall switch click is not enough. Disposals are wired to a circuit that can deliver serious current, and a spinning grinder is a second danger.

Never put your hand into the disposal to test it. Unplug it or leave the power off. If you do not know which breaker controls it, stop and ask a licensed electrician to identify the circuit.

Plug-in or hardwired: how to tell

Look under the sink. A disposal with a power cord ending in a three-prong plug is plug-in. A disposal with a metal conduit or cable going straight into a small plate on its underside is hardwired. Many homes have a switched outlet under the sink, with a wall switch near the sink that you may think is dead.

If the cord and outlet are there and a wall switch near the sink does nothing, flip the other switches before assuming the disposal is broken. A hardwired unit with no switch at all is a different job. If you need help with a jammed or dead unit, garbage disposal repair from a plumber is the usual path.

Installing an air switch

An air switch needs no wiring in the wall. The button is purely pneumatic, and only the module plugs in. Follow the instructions in your kit, since parts vary.

  1. 1

    Turn off the breaker

    Confirm the outlet is dead with a tester before you unplug the disposal.

  2. 2

    Drill the countertop hole

    The button usually needs a hole about 1/2 to 1 inch wide, so check the kit. Granite and quartz need a diamond bit and a pro.

  3. 3

    Mount the button

    Thread the air tube down through the hole and snap the button nut into place.

  4. 4

    Connect the tube to the module

    Push the tube onto the nipple on the module under the sink. Keep the tube without kinks.

  5. 5

    Plug in and test

    Plug the module into the outlet, plug the disposal into the module, turn the breaker on, run water, and press the button.

Permits and licensing for Mauldin kitchens

The City of Mauldin Building and Zoning office says a permit is required before you add, alter, or replace any plumbing system, and contractors must be licensed, bonded, and insured. Whether swapping a disposal or adding new switch wiring needs a permit is a question for the office at (864) 234-3475, since the city's permit page does not spell it out.

South Carolina licenses residential plumbers through the Residential Builders Commission under the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR), and the lookup is public. Electrical work is a separate trade, so ask the electrician for their credentials and check with LLR which board covers them.

That means the honest split is simple. A plumber handles the disposal body, the drain connection, and the dishwasher inlet. A licensed electrician handles new wiring, new circuits, and switch boxes in the wall.

When to call an electrician, and when a plumber

Two different trades own two different problems.

  • ✓Call an electrician if you need a new cable run through a wall, a new circuit, or a new switch box.
  • ✓Call an electrician if the outlet under the sink trips a breaker or sparks.
  • ✓Call a plumber if the disposal leaks, hums but will not spin, or drains slowly.
  • ✓Call a plumber if the disposal must be removed, replaced, or reconnected to the drain.
  • ✓Call both if you are replacing a hardwired unit and also adding a switch.

Not sure whose job it is? Call (855) 575-2890 and describe it. A plumber can handle the disposal and tell you honestly when electrical work is needed.

Wiring a wall switch: what the job involves

A new switch for a hardwired disposal means a single-pole switch on a 15 amp or 20 amp circuit, with the load wire running to the disposal's junction box. A typical home disposal runs on a half to one horsepower motor and draws several amps. The cable must be rated for the circuit and protected where it enters the box.

Fishing cable through a finished wall is the part that goes wrong. It can hit a stud, a plumbing line, or another wire. That is the reason an electrician quotes it as a job, not a part. Kitchen circuits often also need ground fault protection, and a licensed electrician knows what applies in your home.
