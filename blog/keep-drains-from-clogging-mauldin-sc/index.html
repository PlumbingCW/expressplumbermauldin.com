Keeping Your Drains Clear: Habits That Actually Work

By the Mauldin Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

Most clogs in a Mauldin, SC home come from a short list of things going down the drain: grease, food scraps, hair, wipes and soap scum. Keep those out and run a simple monthly routine, and you will avoid the bulk of drain calls.

Where clogs start and how to stop them

Each drain in the house has its own usual culprit. Match the habit to the drain.

The kitchen is where most clogs begin

Grease goes down the drain as a liquid and sets as it cools, coating the pipe wall a little thicker each week. Hot water does not fix this. It moves the grease a few feet downstream, where it hardens again.

Scrape plates into the trash, wipe greasy pans before washing, and keep a jar for used cooking oil. Coffee grounds and eggshells look harmless but they clump and settle in the trap. A mesh sink strainer costs a few dollars and catches most of it. If a kitchen line has already slowed, a plumber can clear the drain before it stops completely.

Garbage disposals do not solve this. They chop scraps into smaller pieces, but fibrous items like celery, potato peels and rice still collect in the bend below the sink. Run plenty of cold water while the disposal works and for several seconds afterward, so the pieces travel past the trap.

A ten-minute monthly routine

  1. 1

    Clean the strainers and stoppers

    Pull every sink and tub stopper and remove the hair and gunk. Rinse them under hot water.

  2. 2

    Flush each drain with hot water

    Run hot tap water for a minute or two. This helps with soap film, but it will not dissolve grease.

  3. 3

    Use a gentle enzyme cleaner if you like

    Enzyme products digest organic film over time. They are maintenance, not a fix for a full clog.

  4. 4

    Check for slow spots

    Run the tub and sink at once and listen for gurgling. Note any drain slower than the others.

  5. 5

    Look at the outdoor cleanout

    Make sure the cap is in place and the area is clear. Do not open it unless you know what you are doing.

Whose pipe is it? The Mauldin sewer split

City of Mauldin Public Works Committee materials from October 2024 describe the city system as consisting only of the mainlines and manholes within street rights-of-way and utility easements. The lateral, the pipe running from your house to that main, belongs to and is maintained by the property owner.

In practice, that means a clog in the yard portion is your bill, whether your street is Corn Rd, Laurens Rd or a side street off E. Butler Rd. Keeping grease and wipes out helps, and so does keeping big roots from growing over the line.

Source type: City of Mauldin Public Works Committee agenda, October 2024.

Chemical drain cleaners: use caution

Caustic liquid cleaners can sit in a stuck pipe, soften plastic fittings and burn skin if the water splashes back. A plumber who arrives to a pipe full of lye has to work around it. If a cleaner fails, tell the plumber what you used and how much.

Safer first moves are a plunger, a hand auger or a wet-dry vacuum. Never mix two cleaning products, since some combinations release toxic gas.

Habits worth building in every room

  • ✓Never pour cooking grease, oil or fat down any drain.
  • ✓Use a mesh strainer in the kitchen sink and a hair catcher in the shower.
  • ✓Flush only toilet paper, even if the label says flushable.
  • ✓Run cold water for a few seconds when using a garbage disposal.
  • ✓Keep tree and shrub roots away from the line between house and street.
  • ✓Treat a slow drain within a week instead of waiting for a full stop.

DIY or plumber: where to draw the line

A plunger or a hand auger handles most single-fixture clogs. If you can clear it in two tries and it stays clear, you are done. If it comes back within days, there is something farther down the pipe.

Call a plumber when more than one drain is affected, when water backs up into a tub or floor drain, or when a sewage smell appears. Those are signs of a main line problem. A plumber can use a cable machine for ordinary blockages or hydro jetting when the pipe walls are coated with grease.

Repeat clogs mean a pipe problem

If a drain clogs more than twice in a year, stop treating it as bad luck. A camera inspection shows whether the pipe has roots, a bellied section that holds water, or a crushed joint.

If you live toward Greer or Taylors, the dispatch line covers those towns too. Wherever the house sits, what matters is the real condition of the pipe, and only an inspection shows that.

Ask the plumber to show you the camera footage. You should see the pipe wall, any roots entering at joints, and any low spots where water sits. That footage tells you whether a cleaning will hold or whether a spot repair is the better spend.
