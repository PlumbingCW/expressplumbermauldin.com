What a Tankless Water Heater Install Really Costs

By the Mauldin Express Plumbing team · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

Most homeowners in Mauldin, SC should plan on a typical U.S. range of about $2,500 to $6,500 installed for a tankless water heater. Simple swaps land near the bottom, and homes that need a bigger gas line, new venting, or electrical work land near the top.

Typical installed cost, by job type

These are typical U.S. ranges, not quotes. The real number depends on your house, your fuel, and the site visit.

Equipment alone commonly runs $600 to $2,000. The rest is labor, parts, and permit.

The unit is only part of the bill

A tankless heater heats water as it flows, so it demands a lot of energy all at once. A gas model can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU per hour, much more than a typical 40 gallon tank at 30,000 to 40,000. That demand is why the gas line, vent, or electrical supply so often needs to change.

Sizing the unit also matters. For a fuller treatment of flow rates and temperature rise, a plumber who works on tankless water heaters will ask how many showers and appliances run together.

The line items that move the price

Quotes that look far apart usually differ on these items.

Gas lineMany older tank heaters run on a half inch line that cannot feed a tankless unit. Upsizing to three quarter inch pipe over a longer run can add several hundred dollars, and long runs more.
VentingNon-condensing tankless units need stainless steel vent pipe built for hot exhaust. Condensing units use PVC but need a condensate drain. Replacing a flue through a roof costs far more than a short wall exit.
ElectricalGas units still need a nearby 120 volt outlet. Electric tankless models may need several two-pole breakers and heavy wire, and some panels cannot supply that without an upgrade.
Permit and inspectionPermit fees vary by jurisdiction, commonly in the tens to a few hundred dollars. The permit also means someone checks the gas and vent work.
Removal and add-onsHauling the old tank, adding isolation valves for flushing, and a pressure relief line are common extras.

Insurance generally does not pay for planned upgrades, and coverage varies by policy. For a number tied to your home, call (855) 575-2890.

Permits and household facts for Mauldin

The City of Mauldin Building and Zoning office says a permit is required before you add, alter, or replace any plumbing system. Contractors must be licensed, bonded, and insured and have the permit before starting. The office sits at 5 E. Butler Rd and can be reached at (864) 234-3475.

That means a quote that skips the permit is a warning sign. A tankless install changes gas, vent, and water connections, so the permit belongs in the price from the start.

A smaller household often needs a smaller unit than a house of five. A $4,000 to $6,000 install is also a real share of that median value, so ask for a payback estimate rather than a sales pitch.

How to compare tankless quotes

  1. 1

    Give every plumber the same facts

    Share fuel type, current heater location, number of bathrooms, and how many fixtures run at once.

  2. 2

    Ask what is in the price

    Unit model, vent material, gas pipe size, electrical work, permit, haul-away, and flush valves.

  3. 3

    Confirm the permit

    Ask who pulls it and whether the plumber is licensed, bonded, and insured as Mauldin Building and Zoning requires.

  4. 4

    Ask about the gas and vent plan

    A written plan with pipe sizes beats a verbal yes.

  5. 5

    Compare payback honestly

    Tankless units often last about 20 years versus 10 to 12 for a tank. Energy savings depend on use, so ask for assumptions.

Running costs after the install

A tankless unit has no tank to keep hot, so it avoids standby loss. Gas models burn fuel only while water flows. Electric models draw heavy current for short bursts, which can raise the demand on your panel.

Plan for a yearly flush with vinegar or a descaling solution run through the isolation valves. A flush takes about an hour and clears mineral sediment from the heat exchanger. A neglected unit loses efficiency and can throw error codes. Budget about $100 to $200 for a plumber to do it, or less if you do it yourself on a unit with service valves.

When a tank replacement makes more sense

If the gas line is undersized, the vent path is hard, and the budget is tight, a new tank may be the better call. A tank swap often costs far less than a tankless conversion. A plumber who handles water heater replacement can price both side by side.

Tankless units are a good fit for homes that want endless hot water and steady use. For occasional use, the extra cost may not repay.

Questions to ask before you sign
