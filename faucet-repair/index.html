Emergency Faucet Repair in Mauldin, SC

Emergency faucet repair in Mauldin, SC means stopping water that will not stop, then fixing the faucet, the valve under it, or both. Our line is open 24/7. While you wait, turn the small oval handle under the sink clockwise, or close the main shutoff if that handle breaks.

Faucets look simple, but the parts behind the handle fail in predictable ways. A plumber finds which part and replaces only that.

A faucet or supply valve is spraying right now

A broken supply line or a snapped stop valve can put several gallons a minute onto a cabinet floor. These steps cost nothing and limit the damage until a plumber is there.

Close the stop valve

Turn the oval handle on the pipe under the sink clockwise until it stops. Do it for hot and cold.

Use the main if it breaks

If the stop valve spins loose or leaks from its stem, close the main shutoff where the line enters the house.

Move what is stored below

Pull out cleaners, paper goods and the trash can. Lay towels to slow the spread to flooring.

Keep power away

If water reaches an outlet or a disposal plug, leave that circuit off at the panel until it is dry.

Then call (855) 575-2890. Evening and weekend calls reach the same dispatch as daytime calls, and a same-day visit is offered when a plumber is open.

Repair the faucet or replace it?

Most faucets do not need to be thrown away. The table shows how a plumber usually decides.

A faucet over 15 years old that needs its second repair is usually cheaper to replace than to keep patching.

Why Mauldin faucets rarely die from scale

Greenville Water's monthly report for March through May 2026 shows total hardness averaging 5.7 mg/L. That is very soft water. Mineral crust inside aerators and cartridges builds slowly here, so a faucet that sticks or sputters usually has a worn seal or a failed part instead of a clogged one.

That changes the first guess. A plumber in Mauldin is more likely to reach for a cartridge kit than for a descaling soak. If a neighbor's faucet clogs often, the cause may be debris from pipe work, not minerals in the supply.

How a plumber finds the failed part

Diagnosis takes minutes when it follows an order.

  1. 1

    Isolate the fixture

    Close the stop valves and open the faucet to confirm the leak stops. If it keeps running, the stop valves are the problem.

  2. 2

    Check where the water shows

    Spout, handle base, mounting deck and supply line each point to a different part.

  3. 3

    Pull the cartridge or stem

    Wear, scoring and brittle rubber are visible once it is out.

  4. 4

    Test the stop valve

    A valve that will not fully close, or weeps when turned, gets replaced at the same visit.

  5. 5

    Run it under pressure

    Water is turned back on slowly and the connections are dried and watched for slow drips.

Signs the problem is the valve, not the faucet

Many faucet calls turn out to be about the shutoff valve underneath.

  • ✓The stop handle turns freely and nothing changes.
  • ✓You see green or white crust on the valve body.
  • ✓Water beads on the braided line near the nut.
  • ✓The handle has not been turned in years and refuses to move.
  • ✓A wet ring appears on the cabinet floor with the faucet off.

Stop valves, especially older multi-turn types, can break when forced. If one will not turn, leave it and call.

A Mauldin leak test before you blame the faucet

Greenville Water maintains the line up to the meter, and the line leaving the meter is the customer's to maintain. If water runs after you fix a faucet, the leak may sit between meter and house. Read the meter, use no water for at least 15 minutes, and read it again.

If the dial moved with every fixture off, you need leak detection on the line more than a new faucet. Greenville Water customer service is at (864) 241-6000 for meter questions.
