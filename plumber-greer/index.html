One utility for water, sewer, power and gas

The Greer Commission of Public Works reports more than 24,500 water meters in the city and beyond it, within a service area bounded by Highway 14, Highway 101 and the Enoree River. Water comes from Lake Robinson and Lake Cunningham, fed by the South Tyger River. The same commission runs the sewer.

That arrangement helps you in an emergency. One utility owns the street-side water and sewer, so a pressure drop on your whole block is a single call. Ask Greer CPW where its responsibility ends and yours begins, because we do not assume that line. Questions about mineral content belong in its annual water quality report.

Because the commission also sells natural gas, a gas smell is a call to 911 or the gas utility from outside the house. Plumbing waits until the building is clear.

Trade Street, Depot Street and the old rail line

Downtown Greer grew around the railroad. The first post office sat in the train station called Greer's Depot, and Greer Station still hosts events beside Trade Street and Depot Street, next to an active Norfolk Southern line. The covered bridge built between 1909 and 1911 is the only one left in South Carolina.

Buildings from that era often keep their original drain routes, and shops and restaurants add grease and wipes on top. A restaurant kitchen or a street-front shop needs commercial plumbing help, and a camera run is the fastest way to learn what is under an older slab.

When a main breaks, the call is not yours

A 2024 break put nearly 10,000 Greer CPW customers under a precautionary boil advisory. A valve installed in the 1970s failed, repairs finished the same night, and samples cleared in about two days. On Jan 3 and 4, 2018, main breaks at Pelham Rd and E. James St left more than 100 homes briefly without water.

If your neighbors lost pressure too, the cause is probably the utility's main. If only your house is affected, check your own shutoff and call us. After any advisory lifts, follow Greer CPW's notice on flushing taps.

Before we arrive at a Greer address

Spend the wait on these four things.

Check the street first

Ask a neighbor whether water is off or weak. If the whole block is affected, Greer CPW is your first call.

Close the house valve

Shut the main where the line enters the house or at the meter box, then open a low faucet to drain the lines.

Keep electricity away from water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms, the water heater and any flooded appliances before touching them.

Move people and rugs out

Raw sewage on floors is a health risk. Keep children and pets out, and lift rugs and boxes clear of the water.

Then call (855) 575-2890 and give dispatch your street and ZIP, 29651 or 29650.

How a Greer sewer backup gets sorted out

Most backups follow the same path, and the order saves money.

  1. 1

    Stop adding water

    Pause the washer, showers and dishwasher. Each one pushes more into the same blockage.

  2. 2

    Rule out the street

    If neighbors are backing up too, report it to Greer CPW. A utility main clog is its job.

  3. 3

    Camera the lateral

    A sewer camera inspection shows roots, a sag or a collapsed section.

  4. 4

    Clear the line

    A cable handles soft clogs, and hydro jetting scours grease and root hair from pipe walls.

  5. 5

    Repair what the camera found

    A broken section may need a spot repair. We tell you when cleaning alone will not hold.
