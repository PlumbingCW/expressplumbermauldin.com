Planters Row and the Vineyard, Mauldin SC

Plumber in Planters Row, Mauldin, SC

Planters Row is an amenity community in southern Mauldin, SC 29662, with a pool, tennis courts and a swim team. Its houses date mostly from the late 1990s and early 2000s, so many are now 25 to 30 years old.

Planters Row at a glance

Listing records place Planters Row and the neighboring Vineyard at Planters Row in southern Mauldin. Streets that appear in the records include Marsh Creek Drive, Sea Oats Inlet, Trailstream Drive and Golden Crest Court. Census address checks place Marsh Creek Drive and Golden Crest Court inside Mauldin city limits.

Real-estate listing data describes Planters Row homes built between about 1996 and 2002, with lots from about a fifth of an acre to over an acre. Vineyard at Planters Row is listed as its own subdivision, built around 1997. Both are described as amenity communities with a pool, tennis, a playground, a clubhouse and a summer swim team.

The listing source also says Marsh Creek and related drainage features run through parts of the community, and some lots back to wooded areas. Amenities are managed by a homeowners association, but the fee and what the association covers were not published in the sources found. The Mauldin plumbing page lists every service.

What a 25 to 30 year old house means for plumbing

A house finished in 1998 is now about 28 years old. Equipment that has never been replaced is that age too. Tank water heaters are commonly replaced around the ten to twelve year mark, so an original heater in a Planters Row home would be well past that. Many owners will already have replaced theirs, and some will not know.

The rating label on the heater shows when it was made. Faucets, toilet fill valves and the flexible supply lines under sinks wear out on their own schedules. None of this means a particular failure is coming, but a house this age is a reasonable one to inspect on purpose instead of waiting for a leak.

A quick plumbing check for a house built around 2000

  • ✓Read the water heater label and note the manufacture date
  • ✓Look under every sink for drips, rust or swollen supply lines
  • ✓Check that each toilet stops running after a flush
  • ✓Locate the main shutoff valve and turn it a quarter turn to confirm it moves
  • ✓Check hose bibs for drips when the hose is connected

Do these checks when nothing is wrong. Finding a stiff shutoff valve on a quiet afternoon is far better than finding it during a leak.

A wet yard near a creek: drainage or a leak

With drainage features running through the community, standing water in a yard does not always mean a broken pipe. Rain, a high water table and a blocked swale can all do it. The way to tell is the meter. Shut off every fixture, read the Greenville Water meter, wait at least 15 minutes and read it again.

If the meter moved with everything off, water is escaping from the supply side. Leak detection finds where, and water service line repair applies when the leak is on the line between the meter and the house. If the meter did not move, the water is probably coming from the ground or a drain.

Water heaters, sewer and permits

When a heater of that age fails, the choice is between repair and replacement, and a plumber can say which makes sense after looking at it. Replacing a water heater counts as replacing part of a plumbing system, and the City of Mauldin requires a permit first. Building and Zoning is at (864) 234-3475.

A sewer problem in the street goes to City of Mauldin Public Works at (864) 289-8904, or (864) 289-8900 after hours. The lateral from a house to the main belongs to the owner. Planters Row is close to the Simpsonville line, and the Simpsonville page covers that side of the border.
