Hadley Park at a glance

Hadley Park sits close to the I-385 and Butler Road corridor in Mauldin. Streets in the neighborhood include Hadley Commons Drive, Moonstone Drive, Alexandrite Lane, Auburn Top Lane and Junebug Drive. The City of Mauldin's strategic plan names the Hadley Park neighborhood, and a U.S. Census address check places Hadley Commons Drive inside Mauldin city limits.

Real-estate listing data describes Hadley Park as townhomes only, in two and three bedroom layouts, built between 2002 and 2006. Lots average about 0.06 acre because the units share walls and common grounds. The same source says the homeowners association covers lawn care, exterior maintenance, pest and termite protection and trash service.

BridgeWay Station, the mixed-use development at the I-385 Bridges Road exit, is nearby. Its postal address is Simpsonville, not Mauldin, so a Hadley Park address and a BridgeWay Station address are not the same city. Mauldin High School is also close, and listing data says parts of the neighborhood are within walking distance. The Mauldin plumbing page lists every service.

Association or owner: who handles the leak

Townhome communities split plumbing duties in different ways. A common arrangement is that the association looks after the building exterior and shared lines while the owner looks after the pipes and fixtures inside the unit. Hadley Park's own governing documents decide the split, and this site has not seen them.

That matters before you hire anyone. A drip under your own sink is almost certainly yours to fix. Water coming through a shared wall, a roof line or a pipe that serves two units is a different situation, and the association may need to be told first or may be the one to arrange the repair.

Questions to ask the association before you call a plumber

  • ✓Is the leaking line inside my unit, or does it serve more than one townhome?
  • ✓Does the association handle outdoor spigots, downspouts and exterior drains?
  • ✓Do I need written approval before a plumber opens a shared wall or ceiling?
  • ✓Does the association require notice if water reaches a neighboring unit?
  • ✓Does my own policy or the association's master policy cover water damage?

If water is escaping right now, close the shutoff valve for the unit first and ask these questions afterward. Stopping the water protects your home and the neighbors on both sides.

Everyday jobs in a townhome that is about twenty years old

A unit that has never had work done still has its original equipment from the early 2000s. The label on a tank heater shows when it was made, and water heater replacement is the usual answer when a heater that age starts to leak at the base. A slow drip from a tank, a stuck toilet flapper or a faucet that will not shut off are all inside-the-unit problems.

For a faucet or toilet that keeps running, see faucet repair and toilet repair. If a water bill jumps and nothing looks wrong, leak detection can locate hidden water. For a water heater that has stopped heating, start with water heater repair. A plumber can tell you what the job involves once the problem is described.

Water, sewer and permits in Hadley Park

Mauldin is served by Greenville Water, and the utility maintains the line up to the outlet side of the meter. In a townhome community the meter may be individual or shared, so ask the association before assuming which side of the meter a leak is on. Greenville Water customer service is (864) 241-6000.

A sewer problem in the street or a manhole overflow goes to City of Mauldin Public Works at (864) 289-8904, or (864) 289-8900 after hours. The city's sewer system covers the mainlines and manholes in street rights-of-way and easements. Who owns the pipe between your unit and the main depends on how the community was platted, and no source this site found settles that for Hadley Park.

The City of Mauldin Building and Zoning office, at 5 E. Butler Rd, (864) 234-3475, states that a permit is required before you add, alter or replace any plumbing system. Ask whoever does the work who pulls the permit, and check association rules on water heater installation too.
