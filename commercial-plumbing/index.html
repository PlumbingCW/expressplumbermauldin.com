Emergency steps when water is pouring into your business after hours

Commercial calls at night differ from home calls. There is stock on the floor, a landlord to reach and sometimes a tenant next door sharing the same line. Work through these moves while the dispatcher gets a plumber moving.

Close the main

The building shutoff is usually where the water line enters, in a utility room or a meter box. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Cut power to wet areas

Flip the breaker for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel dry. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Protect stock and records

Lift boxes and paper off the floor. Move cash registers, computers and food stock out of the spread.

Call with the facts

Give the building type, what is leaking or backing up, whether the main is shut and who can meet the plumber on site.

Smell gas? Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Hidden leaks that run all night in a commercial building

Commercial leak detection is the biggest reason property managers call outside emergencies. The cheapest test is a meter reading at close and again at open. If the meter moved overnight in an empty building, water is escaping somewhere.

The line leaving the water meter is the customer's to maintain. Greenville Water maintains the line up to the outlet side of the meter, so a leak between meter and building is the owner's repair. Closing valves zone by zone shows which section holds pressure.

Then the plumber narrows it with acoustic listening, a thermal camera for hot lines in floors and a pressure test on the isolated section. See finding a hidden leak for the full method. Evening and weekend visits help, since quiet buildings make faint leaks easier to hear.

Annual backflow testing if your building is on Greenville Water

Backflow is water moving backward from a building into the public supply, which can carry contamination from a boiler, irrigation system or fire line. Greenville Water runs a service-wide Cross Connection Control Program and requires customers whose water presents a backflow hazard to install a device or assembly.

  1. 1

    Find the assembly

    Look near the meter, fire line or irrigation tie-in. A reduced pressure assembly has two shutoffs and a relief valve.

  2. 2

    Hire a certified tester

    Devices must be tested annually per SCDES and Greenville Water standards. Greenville Water does not install or test them, so the owner books it. Ask the dispatcher if a certified tester can be scheduled.

  3. 3

    Test and report

    The tester checks the check valves and relief valve, then submits results through the utility portal. Keep your copy.

  4. 4

    Repair and retest

    A failed assembly is rebuilt or replaced, then tested again before the report is filed.

Grease trap trouble in a commercial kitchen

A grease trap slows kitchen wastewater so fat floats up and solids settle before reaching the sewer. When it fills, fat passes through and hardens in the building drain, usually during the dinner rush.

  • ✓Floor drains drain slowly or gurgle after the dishwasher runs.
  • ✓A sour smell rises near the three-compartment sink or the trap lid.
  • ✓Grease shows up at the cleanout or in the first manhole outside.
  • ✓The trap lid is hard to lift because of a thick fat cap.

Cleaning removes the fat cap, water and settled solids and scrapes the baffles. A common rule is to clean when fat and solids reach about a quarter of the liquid depth. Ask the City of Mauldin Public Works Sewer Division at (864) 289-8904 whether any schedule applies to your kitchen.

If the line past the trap is already coated, hydro jetting clears it. Non-urgent trap cleaning can often be booked same-day when available.
