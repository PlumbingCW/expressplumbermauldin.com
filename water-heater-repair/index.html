Water heater leak: where it comes from and what each source means

A leaking water heater is the most common call we hear, and the source decides everything. A drip from the temperature and pressure valve, the drain valve or a pipe connection is repairable. A leak from the tank bottom is not, because the steel liner has corroded through and no patch lasts.

Dry the tank top with a towel and watch where the first bead returns. If it forms at a fitting, tighten or replace that part. If the base is wet and the tank is past about ten years, price a replacement against a repair that may only last weeks.

Never cap or plug the relief valve. It is the one part that keeps an overheated tank from rupturing.

No hot water tonight: what to try first

These checks take minutes and sometimes solve the call without a part.

  1. 1

    Check the breaker or gas valve

    A tripped double-pole breaker is the classic cause for an electric unit. Reset it once, not repeatedly.

  2. 2

    Press the high-limit reset

    Electric tanks have a red button behind the upper panel. If it trips again, a thermostat or element is shorted.

  3. 3

    Look at the pilot or igniter

    On a gas unit, do not keep relighting if it will not hold. A weak thermocouple is a cheap part, but a draft problem is not.

  4. 4

    Feel the pipes

    Hot outlet pipe but cold at the faucet points to a mixing valve or crossover problem elsewhere in the house.

January in Mauldin and why heaters give out then

NWS Greenville-Spartanburg lists an average January low of 32.1 F and 16 January days at or below 32 F. Cold inlet water makes the burner or elements run longer, and a heater in an unheated garage, attic or closet loses heat faster. Insulating the first few feet of pipe and keeping the thermostat near 120 F eases the load, and it is the time of year to spot a weak element before it quits on a freezing morning.

Soft water changes what a tank flush does

Greenville Water's year-to-date average hardness is 5.7 mg/L, which is very soft. Scale on elements and burner chambers builds slowly here, so a dead element is less likely a scale problem and more likely plain age or a dry-fire. Sediment still collects from the supply line.

A yearly drain of a few gallons from the tank bottom still clears what settled, so a tank flush is cheap upkeep, though it matters less than in mineral-heavy areas. Tankless units follow the same logic, and a yearly flush is still worth doing (see tankless water heaters).
