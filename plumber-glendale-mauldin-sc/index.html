Glendale at a glance

Glendale is reached from East Butler Road in central Mauldin. Listing records show streets such as Drury Lane, Hickory Lane and Bangor Street, and a Census address check places Drury Lane inside Mauldin city limits. Records label the area Glendale, Glendale 2 and Glendale III, so you may see all three names.

Real-estate listing data gives a median build year of 1970, with most homes dating from the late 1950s through the mid-1980s. Houses are mostly single-story brick, including original ranches, renovated homes and a small share of new custom builds. The same source records no homeowners association.

Lots are large for Mauldin. Listing data puts the average at about 0.55 acre, with typical lots from roughly 0.4 acre to over an acre. Some properties are described as having detached apartments, finished basements or added wings. The Mauldin plumbing page lists every service.

Remodels and additions change the plumbing

A house that has been added onto is really two or more houses of different ages joined together. A bathroom added in one decade, a finished basement in another and a detached apartment behind the house each have their own supply lines and drains. The original 1960s plumbing may not be the plumbing behind the wall you are worried about.

That makes records useful. Ask the seller, check your inspection report or ask the City of Mauldin Building and Zoning office at (864) 234-3475 about past permits. The city says a permit is required before any plumbing system is added, altered or replaced, so major additions should have left a paper trail.

Questions to answer before a plumber visits

  • ✓Has a bathroom, basement or apartment been added since the house was built?
  • ✓Where does the water line enter the house, and where is the meter relative to it?
  • ✓Do slow drains happen in one fixture, one bathroom or the whole house?
  • ✓Is there a cleanout for the sewer line, and where is it?
  • ✓Do you have inspection reports or permit records for past plumbing work?

Even partial answers help. A plumber who knows which part of the house was added when can trace a problem faster.

Drains, supply lines and larger lots

Repeat clogs in a house with additions usually call for drain cleaning first. If the problem keeps returning or several fixtures slow at once, a camera visit through sewer inspection shows the main line. Where a bathroom or kitchen was added, plumbing installation covers new fixtures and supply lines.

The water service line from the meter to the house is the customer's responsibility. If it leaks, the work falls under water service line repair. A wet spot in the yard or an unexplained jump in the water bill is where a plumber would start with a meter test.

Meter, sewer and permit basics

To check for a leak, read your Greenville Water meter, wait at least 15 minutes without using water and read it again. If you shut the house cutoff and the outdoor meter still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house. Customer service is (864) 241-6000.

Sewer trouble in the street or at a manhole goes to City of Mauldin Public Works at (864) 289-8904, or (864) 289-8900 after hours. A backup inside the house can involve the lateral from the house to the main, which belongs to the property owner.
